Complete Guide to Egg Powder Manufacturing Business

A Comprehensive Business Setup Manual

1. Introduction to Egg Powder

Egg powder, also known as dried egg or powdered egg, is a dehydrated form of eggs obtained by removing moisture from fresh eggs through spray drying or other drying methods. It retains most of the nutritional value of fresh eggs and offers extended shelf life, making it popular in food processing, bakeries, and for export markets.

Types of Egg Powder:

  • Whole Egg Powder: Made from entire eggs including yolk and white
  • Egg White Powder (Albumen): Contains only the white portion
  • Egg Yolk Powder: Contains only the yolk portion

Key Advantages:

  • Extended shelf life (12-24 months when stored properly)
  • Reduced storage and transportation costs
  • No refrigeration required
  • Consistent quality and easy to use
  • High demand in bakery, confectionery, and food processing industries

5. Manufacturing Process

Step-by-Step Production Flow:

Step 1: Receiving & Storage

Fresh eggs are received, inspected for quality, and stored in temperature-controlled conditions (7-10°C) until processing.

Step 2: Washing & Sanitization

Eggs are washed with water and sanitizing solution to remove dirt, bacteria, and contaminants from shells.

Step 3: Breaking & Separation

Eggs are mechanically broken. Depending on the product, yolk and white may be separated or kept together for whole egg powder.

Step 4: Filtration

Liquid egg is filtered to remove shell fragments, chalazae, and other impurities.

Step 5: Pasteurization

Liquid egg is heated to 60-65°C for 3-5 minutes to eliminate harmful bacteria like Salmonella while maintaining nutritional quality.

Step 6: Spray Drying

The pasteurized liquid is atomized and sprayed into a drying chamber with hot air (150-200°C). Water evaporates instantly, leaving fine powder particles.

Step 7: Cooling

Hot powder is cooled to room temperature to prevent moisture absorption and ensure stability.

Step 8: Sifting & Quality Check

Powder is sifted to ensure uniform particle size. Quality parameters like moisture content, protein level, and microbial count are tested.

Step 9: Packaging

Powder is packed in moisture-proof, airtight packaging (aluminum foil pouches, multi-layer bags, or containers) under hygienic conditions.

Step 10: Storage & Distribution

Packaged products are stored in cool, dry conditions and distributed to customers.

8. Raw Material & Sourcing

Primary Raw Material:

  • Fresh Eggs: Main raw material, sourced from poultry farms
    • Requirement: 10-12 eggs to produce 1 kg of egg powder
    • Quality standards: Clean, uncracked, fresh (less than 7 days old)
    • Average cost: ₹5-7 per egg (varies with season)

Supporting Materials:

  • Packaging materials (aluminum foil, LDPE/HDPE bags, cartons)
  • Sanitizing chemicals for cleaning
  • Labels and printing materials

Sourcing Strategy:

  • Establish contracts with local poultry farms for consistent supply
  • Maintain relationships with multiple suppliers to avoid disruptions
  • Consider backward integration by setting up your own poultry farm
  • Join poultry associations for better networking and pricing

Quality Criteria for Raw Eggs:

  • Clean, intact shells without cracks
  • Weight: 50-60 grams per egg
  • Freshness: Maximum 7 days from laying
  • No unusual odor or appearance
  • Free from blood spots and meat spots
  • Proper candling to check internal quality

11. Packaging & Storage

Packaging Options:

  • Small Retail Packs: 100g, 200g, 500g pouches
  • Bulk Packs: 1kg, 5kg, 10kg, 25kg bags for commercial customers
  • Material: Aluminum foil laminated with polyethylene or multi-layer food-grade plastic

Storage Requirements:

  • Temperature: Below 25°C (preferably 15-20°C)
  • Relative Humidity: Below 65%
  • Store in cool, dry, well-ventilated area
  • Away from direct sunlight
  • Off the floor on pallets
  • Proper stock rotation (FIFO method)
  • Shelf life: 12-24 months when properly stored

Labeling Requirements:
